Evolutionary Process

Each candidate image is encoded as a genome containing translucent triangle genes.

01

Initialise

Generate a population of genomes containing randomly positioned and coloured triangles.

02

Render

Draw each genome to an off-screen canvas to produce its image approximation.

03

Evaluate

Compare generated pixels against the target image and calculate a fitness score.

04

Mutate

Change triangle positions, colours, opacity and geometry to create new candidate genomes.

05

Select

Keep successful mutations and discard candidates that produce a worse approximation.

06

Repeat

Continue the process for thousands of generations as increasingly recognisable structure emerges.

04

Painting With Evolution

The algorithm does not know what the Mona Lisa looks like. It does not recognise faces, landscapes, eyes or individual objects. Instead, it treats the target as a collection of coloured pixels.

A candidate solution consists of many translucent triangles. Their positions, shapes, colours and opacity values form a genome. Small random mutations alter these genes and the resulting image is compared with the target.

Mutations that improve fitness survive. Poorer mutations are discarded. Repeating this simple process many thousands of times allows recognisable features of the original image to emerge without explicitly drawing any of them.
